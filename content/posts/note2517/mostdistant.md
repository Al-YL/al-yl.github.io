The James Webb Space Telescope (JWST) has greatly enhanced our ability to identify the most distant galaxies in the universe. 

The driving factor behind its record-breaking discoveries is not merely its sharper vision, but rather its primary coverage of the near- and mid-infrared wavelengths.

## Spectrum of Galaxies

The spectrum of a galaxy typically exhibits the following characteristics. In the rest-frame, the stellar emission is concentrated in the ultraviolet to near-infrared regime, while dust absorbs the shorter-wavelength stellar light and re-emits it in the mid- to far-infrared. For the stellar component, there is a sharp cutoff around 100 nm; at wavelengths shorter than this, aside from a few isolated emission lines, the radiation is essentially negligible. If, however, the central supermassive black hole is active, there can be significant emission in the X-ray regime (below ∼10 nm), but the range between 10 and 100 nm remains largely devoid of radiation.
